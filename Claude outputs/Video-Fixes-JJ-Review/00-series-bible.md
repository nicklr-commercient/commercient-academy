# Series Bible: Commercient Partner Implementation Series

This is the style and structure guide for a 15-video training series. Every video in the series is built from this bible plus one episode file. Read this first. It decides how every video looks, sounds and connects to the others.

---

## 1. What the series is

**Series title:** Commercient Partner Implementation Series

**Who it's for:** the point person at an implementation partner. We call this person "the partner POC," and in the videos we speak to them directly as **"you."** They are technical: comfortable with CRM administration, SQL, and ERP data. **They already know Commercient and what it does.** What's new to them is implementing it.

**What the series is:** an orientation and training-prep series. Partners learn to implement Commercient **live, with the Commercient team,** on a real customer project. These videos support that learning: one short video before each meeting, so the partner walks in oriented. On their first project they **shadow** Commercient's team through every meeting and spend about **one hour preparing** for each.

**Never pitch Commercient.** Don't explain what Commercient is, why companies connect their ERP and CRM, or the product's benefits. Go straight to the meeting and the work.

## 2. The three stages

Every Commercient project moves through up to three stages. Installation always comes first. Phase 1 and Phase 2 can overlap once the first sync is verified, if Phase 2 is licensed and the customer agrees; the series covers them in order:

1. **Installation (Episodes 1–3).** Connect Commercient to the customer's ERP. Ends when the customer approves the record counts and the project is handed to Phase 1.
2. **Phase 1 (Episodes 4–8).** ERP data flows into the CRM. Customers, contacts, orders and invoices show up for the sales team. Phase 1 is the first promise of every project.
3. **Phase 2 (Episodes 9–14).** Records the customer flags in the CRM flow back into the ERP. For example, a new customer created in the CRM becomes a new customer in the ERP. Only some customers buy Phase 2.

Each stage has a color in the series map:
- **Installation:** navy
- **Phase 1:** teal
- **Phase 2:** light blue

## 4. The episode structure (use it in every meeting episode, in this order)

Episodes 1–14 all follow the same nine beats, with the same names, so a viewer always knows where they are:

1. **Where we are.** Show the series map with this meeting highlighted. Say the episode number, the meeting ID and the stage. Example: "Episode 5. Meeting P1-2: Data Matching. Phase 1, stop two of five."
2. **Previously.** One or two sentences: what the last meeting produced, and how it set up this one. Use the "Previously" text in the episode file.
3. **Why this meeting exists.** The purpose, in plain words.
4. **Who's in the room.** The people at the table, by role. Say whether the customer is there.
5. **What happens.** The agenda, step by step. This is the longest beat.
6. **Your prep hour.** What you do before the meeting: the hands-on tasks and the one question you bring.
7. **What you walk out with.** The output of the meeting and your action items.
8. **Watch-outs.** The two or three mistakes that most often go wrong here.
9. **Next up.** Name the next episode and say how today's output feeds it. Use the "Next up" text in the episode file. End on the series map with the next stop pulsing.

Episode 0 has its own structure, described in its episode file.

**Callbacks.** When an episode builds on something from an earlier one, name the earlier episode: "Remember the customization list from Episode 3? This is where it gets built." Every episode file lists its callbacks.

## 4b. Length: 2–3 minutes per video

Every video runs **2 to 3 minutes**, never more than 4. Episode 0 can run about 3. The episode files hold more detail than a video needs. They're the reference; the video is the short cut.

**The short cut** (use it for every meeting episode):
- **Where we are + Previously:** one sentence together.
- **Why this meeting exists:** one sentence.
- **Who's in the room:** a single meeting card, no narration beyond one line.
- **What happens:** the top 3–4 steps only, one sentence each.
- **Your prep hour:** the one or two most important tasks.
- **What you walk out with:** the one output, in one sentence.
- **Watch-outs:** the single biggest one.
- **Next up:** one sentence.

Use Cobalt Creek for one or two moments only, not every beat. Skip "Callbacks" and "Terms" unless one fits in a single line.

## 5. The running example: Cobalt Creek Supply

The whole series follows **one fictional customer** from the first meeting to the last, so each video picks up where the previous one left off. Always say it's a fictional example the first time it appears in an episode ("our example customer, Cobalt Creek Supply").

- **Who they are:** a mid-sized wholesale distributor. Fictional.
- **ERP:** Acumatica, cloud-hosted. Because it's a cloud ERP, Commercient connects to it **by API**.
- **CRM:** HubSpot. Cobalt Creek **already has companies and contacts in HubSpot**, so their project needs data matching.
- **What they bought:** Phase 1 (customers, contacts, and order and invoice history into HubSpot) and Phase 2 with two processes:
  1. New companies created in HubSpot become new customers in Acumatica.
  2. Deals in HubSpot become sales orders in Acumatica.
- **The people:** their operations manager owns the project; an IT administrator manages Acumatica; a HubSpot admin manages the CRM. Refer to them by role only. Don't invent names for them.
- **The partner:** you, the partner POC, shadowing on your first project.
- **Commercient:** one lead per stage: the installation lead, the Phase 1 lead, the data matching team and the Phase 2 lead. Refer to them by role only. Don't use names.

The episode files say what happens to Cobalt Creek in each meeting. Keep their story consistent: what was decided in one episode stays true in every later episode.

## 6. Voice and tone

- **Plain, direct and practical.** Short sentences. Everyday words. Say the point, then move on.
- **Warm and confident**, like a senior colleague walking a new teammate through a project. Never salesy.
- **Speak to "you,"** the partner POC.
- **Define each technical term the first time it's used in an episode,** in one short clause.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Show the Cobalt Creek example, not general claims.
- **No hype.** Never use these words: delve, unlock, journey, robust, seamless, elevate, game-changer, crucial, deep dive, synergy, best-in-class, revolutionize, cutting-edge.
- **Don't tell the viewer how to feel.** No "exciting," "amazing" or "powerful." Just show what happens.

---

## 7. Visual style

- Clean, flat, modern illustration with simple diagrams and icons.
- **Colors:** white and very light blue (#dce7f1) backgrounds; bright teal (#19a4d0) as the one accent color; deep navy (#1c3b60) for titles; dark gray (#444444) for text.
- Recurring visuals: the **series map** (section 3), a simple **ERP ⇄ CRM diagram** with Commercient in the middle, and a **"meeting card"** that lists who's in the room.
- **Don't draw** the Commercient logo, a mascot, or any vendor's logo (Acumatica, HubSpot, Salesforce and so on). Use plain text labels instead.
- **Don't draw imitation software screens** or specific buttons. Commercient's portal changes often, so show concepts and diagrams instead.

## 8. Terms to use consistently

Use these exact terms, and define them the first time they appear in an episode.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ERP** | The customer's accounting and operations system (here, Acumatica) |
| **CRM** | The customer's sales system (here, HubSpot) |
| **Sync** | Commercient's automatic, scheduled movement of data between the ERP and the CRM |
| **Sync frequency** | How often the sync runs. Agreed with the customer during implementation; the cost is part of their monthly usage |
| **Connection method** | How Commercient reaches the ERP: SQL Sync, ODBC Sync, or API |
| **Sync Agent** | Commercient's small Windows program for on-premises ERPs (SQL Sync and ODBC Sync). API connections don't use it |
| **OAuth** | The secure approval step that lets Commercient reach an API without the customer sharing a password |
| **Level 2** | Commercient's developers who finish setting up API connections after OAuth approval |
| **Record counts** | How many records of each object Commercient sees in the ERP. The installation team checks them and the customer approves them before the project moves on |
| **Sample records** | A small batch of real records sent to the CRM for review. Only when the CRM is empty; when it already has data, the CRM sync waits until matching is done |
| **CRM sync** | The sync from Commercient into the CRM. When the CRM already has data, it's turned on only after data matching |
| **CGI onboarding, NDA, TPAF** | What a partner must complete before joining customer meetings or getting logins |
| **Full sync** | Turning on every object and record in the license, including history (6 months by default) |
| **License** | The list of what Commercient syncs for this customer |
| **Customization list** | The customer's requested changes to the standard setup: extra fields, filters, picklists |
| **Data matching** | Linking records that already exist in the CRM to the right ERP records, before more data flows in |
| **Key field** | The ERP identifier stored on a CRM record that links the two, such as the customer code |
| **View** | The SQL query that decides which ERP fields go to which CRM fields |
| **Sync log** | The record of each sync run, including errors |
| **UAT** | User acceptance testing: the customer's own users check the data |
| **Process** | One Phase 2 flow, such as "new HubSpot company becomes a new Acumatica customer" |
| **Commercient Import** | The checkbox a user ticks on a CRM record to send it to the ERP in Phase 2 |
| **Commercient Completed / Commercient Message** | Fields that report whether a Phase 2 send worked, and why not if it failed |
| **Result mapping** | Writing the new ERP ID back onto the CRM record after a Phase 2 send |
| **Sign-off** | The customer's written confirmation that a stage is done |

## 9. Rules for every video

1. **Cover only the current episode's meeting.** Use other episodes only for the "Previously" recap, callbacks and the "Next up" teaser.
2. **Stay inside the sources.** If the episode file doesn't say it, don't add it.
3. **No prices, fees, contract terms or timelines** beyond the ones in the episode file.
4. **No real company names** for customers or partners. Cobalt Creek Supply is the only example customer.
5. **No staff names.** Use roles.
6. **No exact screen or button names** unless the episode file gives them.
7. **Frame every meeting for the shadow project:** on your first project, Commercient runs the meeting and you shadow, prepare and take on small pieces. Close the "What you walk out with" beat by saying what you'll own when you lead this meeting on your next project.
